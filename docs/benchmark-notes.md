optunai is a fork of Optuna (GeorgePearse/optunai, branch feat/llm-sampler) that adds a language-model sampler and pruner behind Optuna's own interfaces. This page reports the design and the measurements. Run on 2026-10-08 on a 16-core CPU VM shared with other jobs (load average 50-80 throughout; wall-clock numbers are inflated, costs and trial counts are not).

## design

The fork keeps the `optuna` import path, every storage, the dashboard and every integration. It adds four public types: `optuna.samplers.LLMSampler`, `optuna.pruners.LLMPruner`, `optuna.samplers.Model` (one LiteLLM-backed implementation, duck-typed so any object with `complete(system, user, schema)` works) and `optuna.samplers.Ledger` (append-only JSONL with one line per model call, proposal, fallback, violation, prune decision and trial end, plus an optional sink that writes each trial into trackinizer as an Experiment proving a Belief). One keyword was added to `Study.optimize`: `allow_new_params`.

What the model gets that TPE does not: the code and documentation of the objective (`context`), per-trial breakdowns (intermediate values and user attributes, not just the scalar), the right to propose a parameter the objective did not declare (off by default), and the obligation to state a hypothesis and cite the trials it used as evidence. These land in `trial.system_attrs` under `llm:*` keys, so any storage and the dashboard carry them with no schema change.

What it is not allowed to do: break a distribution (every proposal is validated; out-of-range and off-grid values are coerced and counted as violations; a missing or unparseable value triggers one repair call; a second failure hands the trial to the fallback sampler, TPE by default, and is counted), change the search space silently (new parameters are gated, written to the storage with a declared distribution, and logged with their rationale), prune less than the classic pruner (`LLMPruner` runs `MedianPruner` as a floor unless `aggressive=True`), or spend without a record (every call is in the ledger with prompt hash, model, tokens, USD and latency). The first trial and any conditional parameter are sampled by the fallback sampler. Prompts are versioned files; their hash is in every ledger line.

## synthetic

Three functions, minimised, 4-D Ackley and 4-D Rosenbrock plus a mixed int/log-float/categorical toy. The `context` for the LLM arms is the one-line description Optuna's own docs give, which for Ackley and Rosenbrock states where the optimum is. That makes the context arm an upper bound (the model can read the answer), so the fair comparison on these functions is the no-context arm against TPE and CMA-ES.

## sklearn

HistGradientBoostingClassifier, 7-dimensional mixed space, 2-fold macro F1, on sklearn's offline `digits` (1797 rows, 10 classes) and `breast_cancer` with 20% label noise. Context for the LLM arm: dataset shape, class counts, the note that labels are noisy, and the search-space documentation. Per-fold F1 is attached as a user attribute so the model sees the breakdown.

## fewshot

The in-house target: a softmax MLP head over cached C-RADIO v4 and DINOv3 crop embeddings from the `visia_data_curve` worlds (municipals and vmi_ewaste, 20,000-crop pools, eight frozen query pools of 200 candidates per dataset). A trial trains the head on 80 or 400 randomly labelled crops for 8 seen cases (2 datasets x world seeds 0,1 x 2 budgets) and scores mean macro average precision; 4 held-out cases (world seed 2) are scored only for each arm's final pick. The 13-dimensional space covers which embedders to stack and their relative weight, centring, width, activation, dropout, learning rate, weight decay, epochs, step floor, batch size, class-weight exponent and label smoothing. The context arm reads the objective code, the few-shot and data-curve READMEs, and the head code from the data-curve benchmark; the open-vocabulary arm may also add one of five optional knobs the objective reads with `trial.params.get`. PCA whitening was left out of the space because the SVD took minutes on this shared box.

## pruner

Same gradient-boosting problems, grown with `warm_start` in chunks of 10 iterations so each trial reports up to 15 intermediate macro-F1 values. TPE sampler, same seeds, three pruners: none, `MedianPruner`, and `LLMPruner` (Jev typed-evaluation judge, median floor, asked every third step after a 3-step warm-up). Every pruned trial is re-run to completion afterwards; a prune is false when the full value would have beaten the study's final best.

## verdict

**Pruner.** On both gradient-boosting problems the LLM pruner (Jev judge on top of the median floor) stopped 102-113 trials against 45-67 for the median pruner, spent 29-35% fewer boosting steps than the median pruner (1,950 vs 2,300 on digits, 1,810 vs 2,770 on noisy breast cancer; no pruning: 2,760 and 3,500), with a false-prune rate of 2% (2 of 102 and 2 of 113 pruned trials would have beaten the study's final best) against 3% and 9% for the median pruner. The price was a slightly lower final best in the median over seeds (0.9703 vs 0.9722 on digits, 0.7637 vs 0.7692 on noisy breast cancer), inside the interquartile range of the no-pruning arm in both cases. Judge cost: about 150 Jev calls per 5-seed set at roughly $0.00006 each, so under a cent per study.

**Synthetic functions.** Every LLM arm, with or without context, lands on the exact optimum of 4-D Ackley and 4-D Rosenbrock at trial 2 (its first proposal), where TPE needs all 30 trials to reach 12.8 on Ackley and 328 on Rosenbrock and CMA-ES reaches 9.7 and 353. The no-context arm does it too: the model recognises these textbook functions from four coordinates and a value. That is recall, not search, so these two problems say little about optimisation. The mixed toy (an unpublished function with a log-scale float, an int, two categoricals and a stepped float) is the fair synthetic test, and there the no-context arm (Gemini 3.5 Flash, numbers only) beats TPE's final best at trial 4 and reaches the exact optimum of 0 by trial 12 in the median over seeds, where TPE ends at 1.09 [0.70, 1.62] and CMA-ES at 0.72 [0.61, 0.88] after 30 trials. Its hypotheses show how: it changes one factor at a time ("keeping the parameters of the best trial constant and only changing the activation"), which is the right strategy for an additive function and one TPE cannot state or follow. The context arm, which is given the source with the optimum in its docstring, reads the answer at trial 2.

**Gradient boosting.** On noisy breast cancer the LLM arm (Gemini 3.5 Flash) reaches TPE's final best at trial 9 (no context) and 15 (context) and ends at 0.7566 against TPE's 0.7516 (interquartile ranges overlap: [0.7516, 0.7589] vs [0.7510, 0.7624]). On digits it loses to TPE and to random search: 0.961 against 0.969 and 0.967, with a tight IQR, because the model settles on one region of the space by trial 10 and keeps refining it while TPE and random keep covering the space. Context (dataset shape, class counts, the note about label noise, the space documentation) made no measurable difference on either dataset. Cost: about $1.1 per 30-trial study, $0.035 per proposal, 14-15 s per call.

**Few-shot MLP head (the in-house target).** The objective has little headroom: random search reaches 0.465 in 40 trials, TPE 0.470, and the best configuration found by anyone is 0.473. Inside that band the ranking is clear. The open-vocabulary arm (Gemini, context, `allow_new_params=True`) is the best sampler: 0.4725 [0.4720, 0.4728] against TPE's 0.4704 [0.4663, 0.4710], with non-overlapping interquartile ranges, and it reaches TPE's final value at trial 8. In 4 of 4 seeds its first proposal added `ensemble=3` (averaging three seeded heads), then it tried `prototype_mix`, `input_noise` and `mixup_alpha`; what stuck was Gaussian input noise of 0.05-0.075 in three seeds and mixup 0.15-0.2 in one. These are the knobs the objective's docstring lists as optional, so the model is reading the code, not inventing; a human would recognise every one of them. Context without new parameters (Gemini) ends level with TPE (0.4698 vs 0.4704) but is far ahead early: 0.4686 at trial 5 against TPE's 0.4521, because the first proposal already applies what the README and head code say (centre, concat both embedders, GELU, lr 2e-3). Claude Sonnet 5.5 with context (one seed only; OpenRouter credit ran out) reached 0.4721 by trial 10 and TPE's final value at trial 2. Context is worth about +0.002 over the numbers-only arm (0.4698 vs 0.4674), roughly half the random-to-TPE gap. Qwen 3.7 Plus with context (5 seeds) ends at 0.4716 [0.4688, 0.4716], above TPE's median and second only to the open-vocabulary arm, with the highest held-out score of the five-seed arms (0.5673), at $0.02 per proposal but a median latency of 127 s per call, five to ten times slower than the other models. The model axis therefore reads: Gemini 3.5 Flash 0.4698, Qwen 3.7 Plus 0.4716, Claude Sonnet 5.5 0.4721 (one seed), all within 0.003 of each other and of TPE. Held-out cases (world seed 2): every optimiser's final pick scores 0.560-0.562 against random's 0.555 (Claude 0.569, one seed), so no arm overfits the seen cases more than another. Cost per 40-trial study: Gemini $1.7 without context, $3.4 with, $4.1 with new parameters ($0.04-0.10 per proposal, 15-27 s per call); Claude $3.6 ($0.09 per proposal, 9 s).

**Reliability.** Gemini via Vertex: 0 fallbacks, 0 coerced values, 0 repair calls over roughly 1,400 proposals. Claude via OpenRouter: 0 coerced values and 0 repairs; its 12-15% fallback rate on the synthetic runs is entirely OpenRouter refusing requests (HTTP 402, in-flight credit reservation) while several studies ran in parallel on a nearly empty account, not a model failure. Qwen via DashScope: 0 fallbacks, 0 coerced values, 0 repairs, 0 call errors; only slow.

**Where an LLM sampler beats TPE, and where it does not.** It wins when the budget is small (the first 5-10 trials) and there is something to read: code, a README, a known function family, or optional knobs it may switch on. It wins as a pruner on learning curves. It loses on a plain tabular space with a decent budget (digits), where it exploits too early, and it costs $1-4 per study where TPE costs nothing and answers in milliseconds. The honest default is TPE with `LLMPruner`, and `LLMSampler(context=..., allow_new_params=True)` for the first dozen trials of an expensive objective that has documentation.

## method

Five seeds per arm, same trial budget per problem, median and interquartile range over seeds. Best-so-far is the running best of completed trials. USD per proposal comes from LiteLLM's price table (OpenRouter and Vertex report it directly; Qwen uses a fixed list price). Fallback rate = trials handed to TPE after a failed proposal, over trials the model was asked for. Violation rate = trials where at least one value had to be coerced. Latency is per model call. Models: Claude Sonnet 5.5 via OpenRouter (no Anthropic key on this box), Gemini 3.5 Flash via Vertex AI with application-default credentials, Qwen 3.7 Plus via DashScope international. Jev via the Vercel AI Gateway. Code: GeorgePearse/optunai, `benchmarks/llm/`.


## ladder (added 2026-10-08)

Three benchmarks for `optuna.ladder`, run on the same shared 16-core CPU VM (load 10-16 during the runs; wall-clock numbers inflated by the 7-9 studies running in parallel, costs and counts are not). Scripts: `benchmarks/llm/rankflip.py`, `benchmarks/llm/fewshot_ladder.py`, `benchmarks/llm/ladder_report.py`; results in `benchmarks/llm/results/ladder/`.

**Rank-flip.** 24 synthetic candidates per pool, each a loss curve `E + A·C^-γ` with `E ~ U(0.20, 0.45)`, `A ~ U(0.2, 2.5)`, `γ ~ U(0.5, 1.3)` and Gaussian noise (SD 0.01) at every rung; rungs 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 that continue one another, so a candidate's compute is the budget it reached. The candidate with the lowest noise-free target loss is "the true best"; it sits at median rank 3 of 24 at the first rung and a third of all candidate pairs change order between the first rung and the target. All arms see the same pool in the same order with the same noise draws. The Ladder uses `n_startup=4`, 90 % intervals, a kill only after two rungs; the rank pruners are Optuna's own `SuccessiveHalvingPruner` (ASHA) and `HyperbandPruner` at η = 2, 3, 4 and `MedianPruner`. The selected candidate is the best observed value among those that reached the target; the metrics are whether it is the true best, its true regret, and the compute spent. 30 seeds.

**Few-shot ladder.** The few-shot MLP head from the first round with three changes: training steps are the fidelity (each head is trained to 25 %, 50 % and 100 % of its step budget and scored at each rung without restarting, so a trial's rungs share a prefix and a killed trial really stops), the head seed and data order come from `trial.user_attrs["ladder:seed"]`, and every study registers the noise floor on a fixed incumbent (concat, centred, 256-wide GELU head, lr 2e-3, 10 epochs; 3 fresh seeds), registers the four held-out cases as the holdout, and after search calls `accept` (fresh-seed re-evaluation of the selected trial on the seen cases, then candidate and incumbent on the held-out cases at the same two fresh seeds) and `near_optimal_check` (two joint perturbations). Four arms: TPE (40 full trials, threshold recorded but unused), LLMSampler alone (Gemini 3.5 Flash, context, `allow_new_params`, no threshold in the prompt), LLMSampler with the frozen threshold in the prompt, and LLMSampler + Ladder (rungs 0.25/0.5/1.0, Jev gate 0.25, the same budget of 40 full-trial units of compute, at most 70 trials). Three seeds per arm (two for the plain LLM arm, which borrows its sibling TPE study's threshold for its own acceptance).

**Noise-floor ablation.** From the same studies: every new best-so-far is a "declared win" in a study with no threshold; each is then tested against the frozen threshold with a `√2·seed_SD` interval (two single runs) and classified `select / insufficient_evidence / defer`. The count of declared wins the rule does not call `select` is the number of decisions the noise floor changes.
